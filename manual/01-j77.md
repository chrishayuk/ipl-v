# §1. J77, written in IPL

*Part One, section 1.0, "Lists and Routines", page 11 (PDF page 46).*

The first routine the manual writes out in full is one of the basic
processes: **J77, TEST if (0) is on list (1)**. Part One builds up to it
from J60 and J2 and then shows it coded, to make the point that most of
the J's could be written in IPL itself and are primitives only for speed.

The code is as printed, except that it is named `U77`: the `J` region
belongs to the system, and `U77` must not replace the real J77. The
comments are the manual's.

```output
U77 S2 YES

U77 S9 NO

```

Both say the same thing. The routine is worth reading slowly, because it
is the pattern of nearly every routine in the manual:

- `40 W0` then `20 W0` keeps the test symbol in a working cell, after
  pushing down whatever a routine above was keeping there.
- `J60` walks the list. Its output, the name of the cell, stays in H0
  as the place reached, and `12 H0` reads the symbol in that cell on top
  of it.
- At the end, `30 W0` gives W0 back, and `30 H0` throws away the cell
  name, so that the routine leaves H0 as it found it except for its
  inputs, which are gone. H5 is whatever J2 or J60 last made it.

`J1`, used in P1, executes the process whose name is (0). It is how a
routine is passed to another as an input, and section 8 builds generators
on it.
